# A corner of the web. Something worth exploring.

Draft for a personal introduction to Periplus.

I keep getting ideas for little data and machine learning projects. Usually, they start with a suspicion: there might be something interesting in this particular corner of the internet.

Then comes the familiar part. Build a crawler. Write some parsers. Extract a dataset. Finally start exploring—and discover that the interesting question needs a field I didn’t collect. Or that my parser threw away something useful. Or that the project wasn’t that interesting after all.

A month later, another idea. Another pipeline.

Periplus grew out of wanting to keep more of that work.

The idea is simple: collect the pages, preserve the original material, and leave yourself room to change your mind. Come back to what a website looked like when you collected it. Try another interpretation. Ask a different question without having to start again.

SQL is how I like to do that exploring. Follow links, look for patterns, pull things into tables, and gradually figure out what I’m actually interested in. The question often takes shape while working with the data.

When something turns out to be worth following, collecting it again opens up another question: what’s changing?

## I’m building this for other people who enjoy doing that.

People who see a directory, a collection of company websites, or an obscure set of pages and think: I wonder what’s in there.

The websites we bring into Periplus are shared. A corner of the web you wanted to investigate becomes something someone else can explore too. They might find a signal you weren’t looking for.

Eventually, I’d like us to share the queries and datasets that come out of those investigations as well.

For now, bring your curiosity—and a corner of the web you think is worth a look.
